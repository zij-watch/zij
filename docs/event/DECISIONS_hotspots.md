# Decisions: hotspots

Lines for `DECISIONS.md`, written on the `feat/hotspots` branch so the two never conflict. When the branch is merged, move these lines to the bottom of `DECISIONS.md` as they are and delete this file. Same rules: time (Cairo), decision, why; a line is never edited afterwards.

- 13 Nov 14:20, filtered FIRMS hotspots are shipped in the app; FIRMS is NASA open data, credited in the README and in every CSV. Until now the repository held only counts. The raw FIRMS files are still never committed.
- 13 Nov 14:20, the shipped hotspots are the kept ones only: `type` 0, MODIS confidence 30 or more, VIIRS nominal or high, outside the static-source mask, on a day the satellite counts as flying. Why: they are the rows the fire cell-days are counted from, so the hotspots on the map add up to the counts in the panel. It leaves out 24 kept detections in Egypt, 38 in Portugal and 1 in Argentina that fall on a day inside a known outage.
- 13 Nov 14:20, hotspots are shipped for the whole of Egypt (173,938) and Portugal (234,254), and in Argentina only inside the Chilecito and Parana Delta preset boxes with 0.5 degree around each (28,857 and 338,675). Why: Argentina whole is 5.9 million kept detections. A drawn area elsewhere in Argentina keeps its weekly counts from the cell files and shows no single hotspots; the app says so.
- 13 Nov 14:20, the hotspot files are gzipped binary columns, one file per region: Egypt 1.48 MB, Portugal 1.81 MB, Chilecito 0.24 MB, Parana Delta 2.63 MB, 6.15 MB in all. Per hotspot: latitude and longitude (whole 1e-5 degree units), date, time UTC, satellite (so the sensor), confidence, FRP, day or night, and standard or near-real-time processing. A file that would pass 5 MB is cut into whole-year periods; none does today. Format: `docs/event/HOTSPOTS_FORMAT.md`.
- 13 Nov 14:20, the app loads the hotspot files lazily: nothing until the day view or the hotspots download is opened, then only the region the area is in.
- 13 Nov 14:20, a hotspot belongs to an area when the centre of its 0.1 degree cell is in it, for boxes and polygons alike. Why: every count in Zij follows that rule, so the map, the CSV and the panel agree. A few hotspots can lie just outside a drawn outline; the CSV header says so. The exact outline is an option in the code (`by: "point"`), not used by default.
- 13 Nov 14:20, in the per-day summary a satellite that was not flying has no count, never zero, and a sensor with no satellite flying has none either. The outage days travel in `hotspots/index.json`.
- 13 Nov 14:20, the hotspots CSV has the columns latitude, longitude, acq_date, acq_time_utc, satellite, sensor, confidence, frp_mw, daynight, version. Its header credits NASA FIRMS with the FIRMS acknowledgement, names the four filters in order, and says that these are detections, not fires, and that MODIS and VIIRS counts do not compare. VIIRS confidence is written as nominal or high; version is SP or NRT.
- 13 Nov 14:20, in the day view the week and the month that hold the day are compared with their historical median from the area's document, the same one the panel reads, so presets and drawn areas work alike. For the running month the days so far are compared with the same days of past years (`this_month`), not with whole months.
- 13 Nov 14:20, checked: for 9 named areas and date ranges the counts made in the browser code equal the pipeline's own daily table, per day and per satellite; for 4 areas they equal the weekly counts of the exported area files and of the cell files over 53 weeks.
